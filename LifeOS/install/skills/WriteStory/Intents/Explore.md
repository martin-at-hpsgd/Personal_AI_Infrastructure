# Explore

**Contents:** Ideal State · Anti-criteria · Output format · Examples

Creative divergence engine: generates fresh, original options for any story element (characters, plot twists, world details, mystery structures) drawn from several genuinely different approaches.

## Ideal State

- Every option fits what the Story Bible (when one exists) already decides, read before any idea was generated: the constraints, the tone/genre, the fixed characters and plot points, the sacred flaw and thematic direction.
- The exploration target is explicit: which layer, what constraints exist, and how wild it should get (conservative variations vs. radically different approaches).
- The options span genuinely different approaches, not a single line of thought: fresh combinations of known-great story elements, constraint reversal (the opposite of the expected), cross-genre pollination (what a storyteller from another genre would bring), and where it helps a `BeCreative` MaximumCreativity deep dive on the most promising angle. Parallel agents are one way to get there, never a requirement.
- Every idea shown went through the `AntiCliche.md` freshness checks (Anti-criteria); any that failed one is flagged, with a fresher alternative pushed for.
- The writer receives the best ideas in the Output format shape: three options and a wild card, each option with the idea, why it works, its risk and its freshness.
- The writer's response decides what happens next: a chosen option is integrated, liked parts are combined into a synthesis, an idea the exploration triggered in the writer is captured and integrated, and "go deeper" develops that direction in detail.
- Once a direction is chosen, the relevant Story Bible layer and its ISC criteria are updated, ripple effects on other layers are checked, and any new gaps the change created are flagged.

## Anti-criteria

- No idea reaches the writer that is the first thing anyone would think of, done in major fiction in the last 20 years, or describable using only genre tropes, without being flagged.
- A writer's "none of these work, but they made me think of..." is never treated as a failure: the exploration did its job, and what it triggered is captured.

## Output format

Present the best ideas to the writer in this format:

```
## Creative Exploration Results: [Target]

### Option 1: [Evocative Name]
**The idea:** [2-3 sentence description]
**Why it works:** [How it serves the story/theme/character]
**Risk:** [What could go wrong with this approach]
**Freshness:** [What makes this NOT the obvious choice]

### Option 2: [Evocative Name]
[same format]

### Option 3: [Evocative Name]
[same format]

### Wild Card: [The Unexpected One]
**The idea:** [The most daring/unconventional option]
**Why it might be genius:** [The upside]
**Why it might fail:** [The risk]
```

## Examples

Exploration prompts by layer.

### Character Exploration
- "What if the sacred flaw was [X] instead of [Y]?"
- "What if the origin wound happened differently?"
- "What if this character's arc was negative instead of positive?"

### Plot Exploration
- "What if the catalyst was [X] instead of [Y]?"
- "What if the midpoint was a false defeat instead of false victory?"
- "What if the ending was bittersweet instead of triumphant?"

### Mystery Exploration
- "What if the reader thinks [X] but it's actually [Y]?"
- "What if the biggest mystery is about [character] rather than [plot event]?"
- "What are five things the reader could be wrong about?"

### World Exploration
- "What unique rule/constraint would create the most interesting conflicts?"
- "What if this world's history had one key difference from the obvious?"
- "What cultural detail would most pressure the sacred flaw?"

### Relationship Exploration
- "What if these two characters had [unexpected dynamic] instead of [obvious one]?"
- "Who is the unlikely ally? The surprising antagonist?"
- "What relationship would most challenge the protagonist's sacred flaw?"
