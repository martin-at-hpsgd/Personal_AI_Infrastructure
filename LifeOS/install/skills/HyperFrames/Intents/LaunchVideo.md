# LaunchVideo Intent

**Contents:** Ideal State · Anti-criteria · Evals · Output format · Inputs · Safety gates · Tool contract · Gotchas

A short, code-built film that announces a product or a release: a launch film cut to music, or a narrated release film in the DA's or the principal's voice. Either way the real product carries it, every shot shows exactly what is being said, and Assay has passed every shot before anyone sees a cut. Composed and rendered on HyperFrames.

## Ideal State

- **The shape is picked before anything is built.** A launch film is cut to music and runs 15–45 s. A release film is narrated and runs at most about 3 min, biggest change first. A narrated tour longer than that, covering every feature, is ContentToAnimation, not this intent. The shape and length are in `Direction.md`.
- **The references are watched, measured and heard.** The pack holds the principal's own reference films (`LaunchFilmReferences.md` in the customization directory) plus any code-built launch films being praised this week. Brand films shot live-action are not references for a code-built film. Every film has a contact sheet, FilmStats numbers and a `WatchFilm.ts` pass, because the sheet shows frames but only the watch pass hears the beat sync and SFX. `References.md` states each film's hook, structure, palette, type, sound and the three techniques worth taking, with links.
- **It is short and cut to music.** By default a launch film's master runs 15–45 s. The music comes first and fixes the tempo, and every picture change lands on the beat grid (60/BPM). An SFX layer gives each on-screen action its own sound. When the film is narrated (a script the principal recorded, or the DA's voice from the voice client), the clean voiceover sets the length and the picture changes land on their spoken words. A release film always carries a music bed under the voice, ducked so that the narration stays at least 15 dB above the music wherever a word is spoken, at a tempo that fits the voice's pace (principal, 2026-10-15). The pace bar below still applies, and the reference motion system carries the picture either way.
- **A release film says how big the release is, and frames its features as the highlights.** It states the release's scale from Deeds: the commits, caps (capabilities), fixes and tends since the last public release, counted by `deeds analyze` on the release's repos over that window, with the numbers on screen exactly as Deeds reports them. Its features are introduced as the biggest of that count, and nothing in the script or on screen implies they are everything in the release (principal, 2026-10-15: a film's close had presented a small share of the features as all of them).
- **A change to a cut is back, checked, in 5–15 minutes.** A new line, a swapped or reframed shot, a re-voice or a full recut reaches a rendered, checked, shareable file through one recut command. That command re-voices only the changed lines, rebuilds the plan and composition, renders on parallel workers, and runs the checks side by side, with judged shot checks reused for every shot whose frames and words did not change. The DA makes the change inline as the one author. A change never fans out to agents (principal, 2026-10-15).
- **The shot list is the film's spine, and it exists before any asset is made.** `SHOTLIST.md` pairs every narrated claim (or, for a music film, every beat) with the shot that proves it. It names the feature, the exact asset (a capture of that feature's own page, a transcript of that feature's own command, or a designed scene of that feature's mechanism), the word or beat it lands on, and the element a push-in or mark lands on. No shot is planned onto a screen that belongs to a different feature.
- **Every asset is checked before it is planned.** A capture or transcript enters the shot list only when it is non-blank, shows a populated working state (no empty, error, "not configured" or failing state unless the line is about that state), is legible at film size, and passes the private scan. A missing or failed asset is recaptured, or replaced by a designed scene of the same feature. It is never swapped for another feature's screen.
- **No shot repeats.** No asset path, and no near-duplicate frame, appears under two different beats, unless the script calls back to it on purpose and `SHOTLIST.md` says so.
- **It looks like the product.** Colours come from the product's own design tokens, read from its stylesheet at build time, and type is only the product's own fonts, embedded from its files. For a LifeOS film that means Pulse's `globals.css` and Pulse's font files.
- **A voiceover is clean before anything is built on it.** The noise classifier and a Gemini listen pass find no throat-clear, cough, click or false start in the clean file; `GateScan.ts` reports zero sample steps on it and on the final encode; and word times were re-derived on the clean file, each snapped to its audio onset. The recipe and its gotchas are in `References/VoiceoverCleaning.md`. A DA voice from the voice client is clean by construction; its word timestamps come from the client.
- **It moves like its reference.** `FilmStats.ts` on the cut and on the chosen reference films meets the bar of the picked direction's genre: kinetic-type films run 2–9 picture changes per 10 s at motion 0.014–0.08, and real-product films run 1–3 at 0.006–0.016 with the camera carrying the motion. A film that looks rich in stills and measures slow is not done; that is the failure this bar exists for.
- **The principal picked the direction from motion, not stills.** Two or three directions each go to the principal as an 8–10 s animatic: the hook and one reveal, rendered on the beat, each anchored to a named reference film. Stills hide pace, so a still-frame storyboard is not a direction pick. The chosen direction, its reference, palette and tempo are locked in `Direction.md`. A release film whose look the principal already approved on an earlier cut reuses that direction and skips the animatics.
- **The real product is on screen.** Every product shot is a real capture of the product used as a plate, from the live app or, when the data is private, from a synthetic install that cannot reach anything real. Only a control that has to move is rebuilt, matched to its plate, following `References/LaunchFilmCraft.md` § "When the product is the point". No number appears on screen unless it is in the facts file or the real UI.
- **Proof holds the brief's facts and marks.** The plan-check exits 0 only when the storyboard keeps every brief fact, such as the price, the offer or the call to action. On the stills and the final encode, every highlight, ring or box encloses the thing it marks, and whatever the brief says the viewer should see is the most prominent thing in the frame. Every highlight is placed from the DOM rect recorded when its capture was taken, through the same transform as the image. `ProofGate` (maintainer-side; not in the public release) refuses to open fresh media without a passing receipt.
- **No text is cut off.** `Tools/TextFit.ts --project <dir> --hits hits.md` exits 0 before any render. The seam check does not catch text clipped by the frame, left in a reveal mask, or covered by a panel. Mark deliberate effects with `data-textfit="ignore"` or `"cover-ok"`, never a caption.
- **Nothing overlaps, misses its target or sits off-centre, and the checks can see it.** Readable text is DOM text; text drawn on a canvas is published each frame in `window.__hfCanvasText = [{text, l, t, r, b}]`. Every connector, leader line or arrow carries `data-joins="#a #b"`, or a canvas one is published in `window.__hfConnectors`, and both ends land within 8 px of what it joins. Every element meant to be centred or aligned carries `data-align` (`center`, `center:#id`, `left:#id`, `baseline:#id`) and holds within 4 px at rest. Words stay inside the title-safe margins (54 px top and bottom, 96 px sides at 1080p).
- **The layout gates are blocking.** Before any film, cut or still reaches the principal, the EvalRound round passes gate L (TextFit every 0.5 s: no occluded, canvas-text, connector, align, clipped or frame finding) and gate Q (two-vendor layout QA on every hit frame). A film that fails either goes back to the builder; `--no-layout-qa` is never used on a film headed to the principal.
- **Every shot matches its words, and Assay proved it.** `skills/Assay/Tools/NarrationMatch.ts` passes on the final encode: no blank or dead frame anywhere, no repeated shot, every narrated sentence covered, and on every shot (not a sample) both vision judges agree that the frame shows the thing being said at that moment. An unavailable judge means the film is not checked, never that it passed. For a music film, the beats in `SHOTLIST.md` stand in for the words.
- **Every builder brief carries the layout rules.** A climb or round brief handed to a builder quotes the two layout bullets above verbatim, plus the shot-list and no-repeat bullets, so the builder knows what gates L and Q and NarrationMatch will measure before writing the film.
- **Every output ships.** The master, a ≤ 15 s ad cut and a seamless ≤ 10 s hero loop for a launch film, plus 9:16 and 4:5 when it runs as an ad; a release film ships its master with captions and a poster frame. Each passed `Skill("ArtDirection")` against a brief naming the chosen reference and the FilmStats bar.
- **A synthetic voice is disclosed.** When the narration is the DA's voice or a voice clone rather than a recording of a person, the delivery hand-off says so and carries a ready caption line disclosing the AI-generated narration, because the seam strips the renderer's metadata and adds no AI-content label of its own (the same rule as `Intents/GeneratedContentVideo.md`).
- **The final encode passed the eval set.** Every master, ad cut and loop rendered with `render --eval` naming this intent, so the Assay video eval set (`video-eval`) measured it minus the checks `## Evals` switches off with their reasons; no check failed, no code check went unmeasured, and the hand-off carries the eval verdict.

## Anti-criteria

- **One author holds the whole film.** A single builder writes the film as one composition with the references open, so pace, palette and motion stay one system. When a builder's time runs out, the next one picks up the same composition from the project files, one at a time. Parallel agents may gather references, capture assets, rebuild UI components or make the music. None of them build segments of the film.
- **No shot borrows another feature's screen.** A gap in the assets is a capture job or a designed scene for that feature, never a stand-in from elsewhere in the product.
- **A film reaches the critique panel while it still measures below its reference.** Before any judge sees a render, its contact sheet sits next to the reference sheets, its FilmStats row meets the bar, and what looked cheap or slow is fixed. The critique panel sharpens a film that already matches its reference and does not pick the direction.
- **No cut reaches the principal, share links included, before NarrationMatch, the layout gates and the critique have run on it.** When the principal asks to see a cut early, it goes with every failing check named in the same message.
- **Explainer defaults do not apply here.** The explainer theme (`Tools/Theme.ts` merged with the install's `Theme.json`) and the explainer sections of the install's `PREFERENCES.md` are for explainers. A launch or release film takes its palette and type from the product and its speed from the chosen reference.
- A narrated walkthrough of every feature, running past the release-film length, is never dressed up as a launch film; that is a different deliverable (ContentToAnimation), and it is how this intent failed before.
- Nothing beyond the animatics is built before the principal picks one direction.

## Evals

- compiled: the Assay video eval set (`video-eval`, `skills/Assay/Tools/VideoEval.ts`), run by the seam's `render --eval` on every final encode with the project passed as `--composition`, so `type-rendered` reads the type the film was built with, plus the checks Assay compiles from the Ideal State and Anti-criteria above.
  - disabled: duration, voice-identity, voice-wer, voice-pace, script-length, visual-variety, detail, card-corners — each reads a VideoEssay run's `Script.json`, narration timeline, voice records or capture list, which a HyperFrames project does not keep, so on its encode they cannot measure.
  - disabled: watermark-brand, watermark-badge, watermark-zone — a HyperFrames composition draws no channel mark and no DA badge corner; where a mark goes is the film's own direction.
  - disabled: format-4k, encode — the master renders at the composition's own frame (16:9, 9:16, 4:5 or square) through the seam's default encode; the 4K x264 CRF ≤ 12 bar is the VideoEssay YouTube master's.

## Output format

A product launch film is the one asset that announces a product. It also runs as paid-ad creative and plays as the logged-out landing-page hero. It is a short piece of motion design cut to music: huge type, the real product as captured plates with only the moving controls rebuilt, one visual idea that flips, and a change of picture at the pace its genre's FilmStats bar sets. A release film is the same craft with a voice: the biggest changes first, each shown on its own real screen or command, a picture change on each named thing, in the product's own look.

## Inputs

- The reference pack: `LaunchFilmReferences.md` in the customization directory. If it is missing, create it with one link per film, or start from the films found this week.
- For a release film, the release analysis: what changed since the last public release, ranked by how much it changes a user's day (the release intent that commissions the film names the source).

## Safety gates

1. After research, show the reference sheets with one line on each. The principal can add or swap films.
2. After the animatics, the principal picks one direction. Nothing else is built before the pick.
3. A release film's script and shot list go to the principal before voicing. The build proceeds unless the principal redirects.
4. After the first full render passes NarrationMatch and the layout gates, show the cut and its FilmStats row next to the pack's before the critique loop runs.

## Tool contract

- References: your X tools for every X read, and Research, to find praised films, each watched at its public page. `bun Tools/FilmStats.ts <films…> --sheet` gives numbers and contact sheets and `bun Tools/WatchFilm.ts <films…>` the watch-and-listen pass (public films, unless the install's `PREFERENCES.md` trusts the vendor with private cuts); both read local files, so they run on your own earlier films and on any film its maker offers as a download. A reference that exists only as a page is studied from the page, and its FilmStats row is marked missing in the pack; it is never downloaded from a host that does not offer the download, and a watermark is never removed. The install's `PREFERENCES.md` may name its own way to stage reference files.
- Music: a licensed track from the music source the install's `PREFERENCES.md` names, or a code-synthesised bed where a scoring tool is installed. The tempo is chosen, so the beat grid is known. A placeholder bed is named as one in the handoff.
- Voiceover cleaning, only when the film is built on a recorded script: the ordered recipe and its gotchas in `References/VoiceoverCleaning.md`. A DA voice comes from `LIFEOS/TOOLS/FishTTS.ts` with word timestamps.
- Product capture: capture the live app at 2x. Log in with the product's sanctioned test identity in the Interceptor test profile, and capture through `Capture.sh --pixel`. The DOM-render default substitutes a fallback font on web-font pages, which widens text and truncates names. Get a phone-width view from a same-origin 390 px iframe inside the test tab, never by resizing a window. For a product whose real data is private, capture a synthetic install in a network sandbox that allows only its own port, record each still's target DOM rects in a sidecar, and reject blank, empty-state and private-scan failures at capture time.
- Proof: the commands are in `SKILL.md` § Tools; the plan file is `STORYBOARD.md` (for a release film, `SHOTLIST.md`), the brief `BRIEF.md`, the kind `video`.
- Shot match: `bun ~/.claude/skills/Assay/Tools/NarrationMatch.ts --video <mp4> --words <word timestamps json> --shots <shots json> [--brief BRIEF.md] [--json]` (`--code-only` for the fast tier while building). Exit 0 pass, 1 fail, 2 unavailable. Every shot in the shots file carries `feature` (what the narration is about) and `shows` (what its asset shows, from the asset audit). A mismatch fails as `borrowed`, which is the one catch the vision judges cannot make from pixels.
- Build and render: `Tools/Scaffold.ts` and the seam `LIFEOS/TOOLS/HyperFrames/HyperFrames.ts` (`check`, `snapshot`, `render --audio`). A final encode adds `--eval ~/.claude/skills/HyperFrames/Intents/LaunchVideo.md`, which runs the Assay video eval set on it, exits 1 on a blocking result and writes `<out>.eval/video-eval.json`.
- Quality: `skills/ArtDirection/Tools/Critique.ts --video <mp4> --brief Brief.json`, `Deliver.ts`, and `Calibrate.ts` for the principal's verdict.
- Round runner: `bun skills/Art/Tools/EvalRound.ts --video --candidates <dir…> --incumbent <dir> --brief Brief.json --bpm <n> --grid eighth --k 3 --out <dir>` scores every film in a round at once (ffprobe, FilmStats, TextFit, seam check, beat grid, Proof, layout L, layout QA Q; table `PFTSBRLQ`), then k Critique readings on one shared seed and the paired keep rule against the incumbent; it writes `round.json`.

### Intent-to-Flag Mapping

| The run needs | NarrationMatch flags |
|---|---|
| Fast check while building, no model calls | `--code-only` |
| The full check on the final encode | `--video <mp4> --words <json> --shots <json>` |
| Shots the tool should find itself (no shot list) | omit `--shots` |
| Product context for the judges | `--brief BRIEF.md` |
| Machine-readable result | `--json` |

## Gotchas

- **A voice-led, one-world film came out slow and was rejected (2026-09-29).** The last run cut its whole picture to one long voiceover in one dim navy wall with one continuous camera. It measured far below the references on both beats per 10 s and motion, and the principal rejected it. Stills were how the direction was picked, so nothing caught the pace until the end. Hence the music-first, animatic-picked, FilmStats-gated shape above (principal directive, 2026-09-29).
- **The previous research pack held the wrong films (2026-09-28).** It was mostly live-action brand films from the model labs and a long commercial. None were code-built SaaS launch films, so the builder had nothing to copy the motion from. The reference pack is where the bar comes from.
- **Scene detection misses motion-design cuts.** Morphs, match cuts and masked reveals do not trip a 0.3 scene threshold. A short film with many shots can register zero hard cuts. Read `beatsPer10s` and `motion`, not `cuts`.
- **An opening on the product feed was rejected on sight (2026-09-28).** A film that opened on a zoomed-in list of stories read as "not a launch video". The hook is the first beat, and the product comes after it.
- **Critique rounds without a picked direction go in circles (2026-09-28).** Round after round the scores crept up only slightly while the judges traded complaints. The principal's pick of one reference is the anchor.
- **Films passed every check and the principal still saw clipped text and no real app (2026-09-29).** Kinetic-type versions rebuilt the UI as styled components and cut words at the frame and at reveal masks. The seam check, FilmStats and contact-sheet reads missed all of it. TextFit now gates clipped text, and real captures now carry the product.
- **Highlight rings sat visibly off their numbers, and the final frame sold the wrong price (2026-09-29).** A real-product film passed TextFit, FilmStats, GateScan and the builder's own still review. The principal caught both defects. The rings were marked `cover-ok`, so TextFit skipped them. The storyboard's last beat said "pricing plate" and dropped the brief's price, so the camera centred on the most expensive tier. Proof plan-check catches the dropped fact before building, and `mark-crosses-text` / `mark-encloses` catch the rings on the first stills.
- **Strike bars sat below the cap middle of condensed caps (2026-10-09), the same class as the 2026-09-29 rings.** Both are a deliberate overlay whose position relative to its text nobody measured. The bars used a CSS `top` percentage of the line box, which put them visibly low on DIN Condensed. Proof's `mark-crosses-text` failed every deliberate strike, so the builder dropped `data-mark` and kept `cover-ok` with a reason, and nothing measured the bars. Declare every strike `data-mark="strike"`: Proof's `mark-strikes` measures it on its word's cap middle (15% of cap height, ≤ 12 px overhang). Place it after fonts load with canvas TextMetrics and a baseline marker. A thin bar crossing text with no `data-mark` now fails `mark-undeclared`, and TextFit fails a text-overlapping `cover-ok` that gives only a reason.
- **The principal saw sliced cap tops on condensed-caps words in one render round that TextFit passed (2026-10-10).** TextFit now flags ink cut by more than 2 px on any side, down from 3% of the ink height (about 12 px on 500 px caps). It also counts `mask-image` ancestors and keeps a slice that moves with its mask, so that run reports one word cut on the left by its mask as it scales. The same run measured the other words' ink well inside their masks, and a mask-on against mask-off screenshot came out pixel-identical, so condensed caps that look flat need a pixel check against the unmasked glyph before anyone calls them sliced.
- **A narrated product data film drew everything on one canvas, so no check saw the collisions (2026-10-10).** Its charts, lines, circles, curves and labels were all canvas drawing. Text sat over drawing, lines missed their targets and items sat off-centre, and the principal caught them by eye. Few of the round's evals touched layout, and those read DOM text only. Hence DOM text or published canvas boxes, `data-joins`, `data-align`, and the blocking L and Q gates above.
- **Per-frame grain makes the master too big to compress (2026-09-29).** A film with grain reseeded every frame came out at over a gigabyte, and even a high-quality re-encode barely shrank it. For the file anyone watches, re-encode with light denoise: `-vf hqdn3d=3:2:4:4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 28`. That cut it to a small fraction of the size with text still sharp and the grain texture intact. Keep the seam master for archive.
- **Parallel helpers share machines.** Give each its own scratch dir, never `pkill` by name, and retry a render that fails with MODULE_NOT_FOUND once, because the shared HyperFrames package cache races.
- **When browser capture of the product fails, fetch the rendered HTML** with the sanctioned test login and rebuild the components from its real markup and data.
- **A release film shown the same screens over unrelated words passed every check (2026-10-15).** The LifeOS 8.439 release film used Today in many shots and put a HillClimb line over the Review page, a texting line over the Skills page and a voice line over Velocity. Several capture PNGs were blank, so the builders borrowed other features' screens to fill the gaps. The ArtDirection critique sampled a handful of frames for look and never compared a frame with its words or with other shots, so it scored the film with zero criticals while the principal called it a failure of the eval system. Hence the shot list, asset checks, the no-borrow and no-repeat rules, and NarrationMatch on every shot.
- **Many parallel segment builders made one film read as many (2026-10-15).** The same release film was split across parallel agents, one per segment group, against this intent's one-author rule. Shots repeated across segments and the pacing varied. Parallel work is for assets; the composition has one author at a time.
- **A short release film took hours to get to a checked cut (2026-10-15).** The work was split across many agents for captures, scenes and several successive authors. Messages sent to finished agents woke them, so two writers edited the same scene or plan more than once, and one overwrote a render that had just been checked. Each agent also spent most of its 30 minutes reading context before writing anything. Edits now go inline through the one recut command, and agents are used only for the first wide asset pass.
- **The release film said its handful of features were the release (2026-10-15).** The close and the "there's a lot more" line read as a complete list, while Deeds counted far more work behind them. The principal called it out, hence the Deeds scale bullet above.
- **The first release-film look was invented, not the product's (2026-10-14).** An art council read a palette from a style guide and never reconciled it with Pulse's stylesheet, so the film came out ember and teal against a deep-blue product, in the wrong fonts, with highlight boxes placed by hand. The principal rejected it. Colours and fonts now come from the product's own files, and highlights from captured DOM rects.
- **A synthetic install leaked the real one into its pages (2026-10-15).** The synthetic Pulse's server fell back to the real Pulse address when one env var was unset, and many tools hardcode that address. Synthetic captures run under a macOS `sandbox-exec` profile that denies all outbound network except the lane's own port. That profile must also allow `(local ip "localhost:<port>")`, because a bare outbound deny blocks `sendfile()` replies and every page stalls after its headers.
