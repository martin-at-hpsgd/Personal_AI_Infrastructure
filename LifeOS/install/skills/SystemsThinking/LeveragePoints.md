# SystemsThinking — Leverage Points Reference

For `Intents/FindLeverage.md`: exceptions to simplistic level ranking.

- LP 12 parameters can have structural effects when they cross contention or queue-formation thresholds.
- LP 11 buffers can decouple failure modes between pipeline stages; oversized buffers impose cost and slow response without fixing flow imbalance.
- LP 10 topology changes may require expensive, slow rebuilding; structural impact is not proof of feasibility.
- LP 9 delay matters relative to the system's rate of change.
- LP 5 rules invite resistance or proxy gaming unless incentives align.
- LP 4 efficiency measures that eliminate diversity, concentrate power, or lock structure can destroy adaptive capacity.
- LP 3 is the actual goal inferred from rewards, metrics, and resource flows, not the stated mission.
- LP 2–3 threaten beliefs and power structures; account for that resistance.

Prefer the highest feasible leverage within the real budgets: time, cost, ownership, and politics where an organization is involved. A tactical intervention can buy time for a structural one. Before committing to any LP ≤ 5 intervention, run **CausalLoop** to examine downstream effects and adaptation.
