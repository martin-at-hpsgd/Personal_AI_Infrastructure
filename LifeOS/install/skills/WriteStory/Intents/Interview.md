# Interview — The Layering Interview

Takes what the writer already has (a line, a folder of notes, a half-drafted chapter) and builds the rest of the story with them, deriving the hidden structure their own material already implies. The deliverable is a saved `interview.md` holding a spine the writer ratified.

## Ideal State

- Every question and proposal names the part of the writer's material it starts from.
- A saved `interview.md` exists at `LIFEOS/USER/CUSTOMIZATIONS/SKILLS/WriteStory/projects/<book-slug>/interview.md` holding every item under Output format: seed class and derivation lens, the confirmed spine, per-layer state for all seven layers, favorites and reader target, and the rough Foolscap spine marked explicitly disposable.
- Every spine element is derived from the writer's seed, not invented, and carries its source label, "inferred from your seed: <their words>" or "a new option I'm offering", so the writer can always tell which is which; every element was ratified or redirected by the writer.
- Every proposal was played back in the writer's own words so they could correct it, offered as two or three contrasting candidates to choose among, and any redirect is recorded as the truth: the writer's word always wins.
- Deepening sits where the writer's energy and material already are: anything of the system's own is offered only after their pages have been drawn on, and a genuinely blank layer is handed to Explore.
- A vibe/image-only seed gets elicitation, not derivation: the spine is built only once texture and image drawn from the writer give enough of their own substrate to build on.
- The derivation lens fits the seed class, genre, and where the writer started, is stated in one line with why, and sets only the order the seven layers are reached; all seven remain the destination.
- An existing project continues from the current state of its `interview.md` / `ISA.md`, re-read first; the saved file is the resume point.
- The session ends with the assembled spine reflected back, what is confirmed vs. still open named, and the next step (Explore, BuildBible or WriteChapter) left to the writer, who drives.

## Anti-criteria

- Never asks the writer anything before reading the material they brought.
- No generic questionnaire that ignores what the writer gave: that is the failure this intent exists to kill.
- Never invents the spine, and never fabricates a flaw and plot from a mood and then asks the writer to "confirm" it: that is substitution wearing a mask.
- Never a single answer to approve in place of options: approving a plausible suggestion is not the same as creating it.
- Never fills a genuinely-blank layer silently.
- Never emits prose: this intent produces a spine, not prose. Only WriteChapter and Revise emit story text.
- Never restarts an existing project.

## Inputs

### Seed classes

Classify what the writer brought; it sets how you proceed:

| Seed class | What they brought |
|---|---|
| **Character** | A person, a flaw, a voice |
| **Premise** | A "what if", a situation, a hook |
| **Plot** | A sequence of events, a structure |
| **Theme** | An argument, an idea, a question to explore |
| **World** | A setting, a system, a place |
| **Vibe / image** | A mood, a feeling, a fragment — no agent or causality yet |

## Output format

A saved `interview.md` at `LIFEOS/USER/CUSTOMIZATIONS/SKILLS/WriteStory/projects/<book-slug>/interview.md` containing:

- **Seed class** and the **derivation lens** used (one line each, with why)
- **The confirmed spine** — every element provenance-labeled (inferred-from-seed vs. offered) and every element ratified or redirected by the writer
- **Per-layer state** — each of the seven layers marked confirmed / partial / open
- **Favorites + reader target** — 3-5 stories the writer loves and what specifically they loved (voice + taste to anchor to); what they want a reader to feel on finishing (becomes a top-line Story Bible criterion)
- **A one-page rough spine** (Pressfield Foolscap: theme, dramatic question, hook, climax, three acts), marked explicitly disposable — a whole beginning-middle-end the writer can see early, with sane defaults offered rather than a meta-decision forced at every turn

**The psychology-first chain** — derive each element with the writer, as a labeled proposal, offering contrasting candidates when unsure:

1. **The false belief** — Storr's sacred flaw / Cron's misbelief, held to precision: not "she's insecure" but "she believes she's the only adult in any room."
2. **Origin damage** — the specific past moment that planted it.
3. **The dramatic question** — falls out of the flaw: *"will they stay [flaw] or become [not-flaw]?"* The engine of the book.
4. **Want vs. need** — want is what they chase; need is usually the inverse of the flaw.
5. **The opposition / pressure** — what forces the question. Not just a villain: a person, system, fate, society, time, institution, or the character's own contradiction — whichever actually presses on this flaw.
6. **Theme, in one sentence** — controlling idea (Pressfield) / premise (Egri) / moral argument (Truby); left open for theme-emergent lenses.

Other lenses derive the equivalent spine in their own order (see `DerivationLenses.md`). The move is always the same: *"You gave me X. That implies Y. Does that land?"* — provenance labeled.

### Handoff

The assembled spine reflected back, what's **confirmed** vs. still **open**, and the next step left to the writer:

- **Deepen a thin layer** → **Explore**
- **Build the full structured plan** → **BuildBible** (turns the spine into the project ISA next to the interview)
- **Start writing the strongest scene now** → **WriteChapter**

## References

### Derivation lenses

No single spine is correct. Full detail in `DerivationLenses.md`.

| Lens | Best for | Spine it derives |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychology-first** (Storr / Cron) | Character-arc, literary character studies | Sacred flaw / misbelief → dramatic question → plot as its test |
| **Structure-first** (Pressfield / Truby) | Genre, thriller, procedural, plot-driven | Hook-Build-Payoff / moral argument → character fitted to it |
| **Premise-first** (Egri) | Argument-driven, idea fiction | Cause→effect premise sentence → characters that prove it |
| **Theme-emergent** | Literary, experimental, slice-of-life | Hold theme loose; let it surface from scenes, derive it late |
| **Resonance-first** | Vibe / image / mood seeds | Texture and image before any causal spine |
